
# Research Questions and Hypotheses

## 1. Research Title

Human Ability to Detect AI-Generated Phishing Emails: A Review of Existing Research

## 2. Research Aim

The aim of this study is to examine how university students identify phishing emails in the context of generative artificial intelligence.

The study combines a review of existing research with an empirical survey. It focuses on participants' ability to distinguish selected AI-generated phishing emails, human-written phishing emails, and legitimate email examples.

## 3. Research Objectives

The study has the following objectives:

1. To review existing research on human phishing detection.
2. To examine how accurately students identify AI-generated phishing emails.
3. To compare participant responses to AI-generated and human-written phishing examples.
4. To explore the relationship between self-reported cybersecurity knowledge and phishing detection performance.
5. To examine the relationship between previous phishing experience and detection performance.
6. To identify the indicators participants primarily use when evaluating email messages.

## 4. Research Questions

**RQ1.** How accurately can university students identify AI-generated phishing emails?

**RQ2.** Is there a difference in participants' ability to detect AI-generated and human-written phishing emails?

**RQ3.** How are self-reported cybersecurity knowledge and previous phishing experience related to phishing detection performance?

**RQ4.** Which indicators do participants primarily rely on when identifying phishing emails?

## 5. Research Hypotheses

**H1.** Participants with higher self-reported cybersecurity knowledge demonstrate higher phishing detection performance.

**H2.** Participants with previous experience recognizing phishing messages demonstrate higher phishing detection performance than participants without such experience.

**H3.** Participants' detection performance differs between the selected AI-generated and human-written phishing examples.

These hypotheses are exploratory and will be evaluated using the collected survey data.

## 6. Operational Definitions

| Concept | Operational definition |
|---|---|
| AI-generated phishing | Phishing emails identified as generated using artificial intelligence |
| Human-written phishing | Phishing emails identified as written by a human |
| Legitimate email | An email example classified as non-phishing in the verified answer key |
| Detection accuracy | Proportion of participant classifications matching the verified email labels |
| Phishing recall | Proportion of phishing emails correctly identified by participants |
| Specificity | Proportion of legitimate emails correctly identified |
| Cybersecurity knowledge | Participant's self-reported knowledge level |
| Previous phishing experience | Participant's self-reported experience with phishing messages |

## 7. Scope and Limitations

The empirical survey contains 12 email examples:

- 3 AI-generated phishing emails.
- 3 human-written phishing emails.
- 6 legitimate emails.

The study evaluates the selected email examples rather than all possible AI-generated or human-written phishing messages.

The number of stimuli in each phishing authorship group is limited. Therefore, comparisons between the groups are exploratory and should be interpreted cautiously.

Self-reported cybersecurity knowledge does not represent an objective cybersecurity knowledge test.

## 8. Relationship Between Research Questions and Analysis

| Research Question | Data | Planned Analysis |
|---|---|---|
| RQ1 | AI-generated phishing classifications | AI phishing Recall |
| RQ2 | AI and human phishing classifications | Participant-level paired comparison |
| RQ3 | Cybersecurity knowledge and previous experience | Group-level descriptive and exploratory analysis |
| RQ4 | First detection indicator | Frequency and percentage distribution |

The analysis will use verified ground-truth labels and participant responses. No results will be reported before the data validation and processing stages are completed.
